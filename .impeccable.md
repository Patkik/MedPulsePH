## Design Context

### Users

Patients and emergency responders need a clear, quick path to the information they are allowed to see. Hospital reception staff, clinicians, band officers, administrators, and privacy officers work across phones and shared desktop workstations, often with interruptions.

### Brand Personality

Calm, clear, and reassuring. The interface should feel dependable and human, with direct language and no decorative claims of clinical readiness.

### Aesthetic Direction

An organized care workspace: warm white surfaces, deep evergreen typography, restrained clinical status colors, deliberate spacing, and a distinctive geometric clasp identity. The UI must adapt to small touch screens and desktop keyboards. Critical content must not depend on color alone.

### Design Principles

- Make the current hospital, patient, role, and session context clear.
- Show a small number of meaningful next actions rather than decorative statistics.
- Treat inaccessible, empty, pending, error, and interrupted states as first-class workflows.
- Keep private and clinical data behind server-enforced authorization; prototypes use synthetic data only.
- Prioritize legibility, visible focus, reduced motion, and accessible authentication.
